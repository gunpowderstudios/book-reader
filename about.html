About E.D.D.i.

His 5-year mission: To take the FREE books of Earth to the edges of the universe.

The little spaceship from Norwood

Somewhere in Norwood, South London, E.D.D.i. is preparing for a rather unusual voyage. His spaceship isn't carrying soldiers, supplies or mysterious alien technology. Its most precious cargo is a library of stories from Earth.

His mission? To seek out new readers, revisit old worlds and share the wonderful books humanity has written — from gothic castles and daring adventures to mysteries, monsters and faraway planets. The journey starts here, with a free book and the press of a Play button.

What is E.D.D.i.?

Behind the spaceship story, E.D.D.i. is a free, open-source EPUB reader created by Gunpowder Studios. It lets you explore classic public-domain books, open your own EPUB files, follow the words on screen and listen to them read aloud by AI narrators — directly in your browser.

There's no subscription or account needed. Books you add are stored in your browser on your device, and E.D.D.i. can remember where you stopped reading. The aim is simple: make great stories easier to discover and enjoy, whether you're reading with your eyes, listening, or doing a bit of both.

Powered by open-source voices

Desktop narration uses Kokoro, with browser-based synthesis via the Kokoro project. We're grateful to its developers and contributors. Explore Kokoro voices.

We're also experimenting with the lighter KittenTTS and KittenTTS Web to help make narration more practical on phones and tablets. Thank you to the KittenML team and contributors. That work remains experimental; some devices may not have enough memory for on-device AI speech.

Free books, responsibly shared

E.D.D.i. helps you discover public-domain works and read books you are authorised to use; it doesn't make every ebook free. Copyright rules differ between countries, and newer translations and editions can have their own rights. You can also open legally obtained DRM-free EPUBs for personal use where permitted.

E.D.D.i. is free to use, and its source code is available on GitHub.
